Lou’s journey

Four years a starter, one round short, twenty five years teaching.

Lou DiChiaro played four years at Seton Hall, captained the 1983 side, hit .321 in the Cape Cod League and was drafted by the Baltimore Orioles. Everything he learned in those years is what he has spent every day since passing on, in a room in Fair Lawn, to players who are at the start of it.

Why the academy exists

He built the room he wished he had.

A four year starter at a Big East programme learns things that never make it into a team practice, because a team practice has twenty players and forty five minutes. Lou opened the academy in 2000 to teach the way he was taught in the cage, one player at a time, with enough reps to actually change something.

That is still how the place runs. Small groups, real reps, and coaches who have already been where the player is trying to go. Seven days a week, year round, because a swing does not keep to a season.

“Lou is the go-to baseball instructor in Bergen County. His knowledge, experience, and respect for the game are unmatched.”
Mike Sheppard Jr., Seton Hall Prep

Still to come

The part only Lou can write.

Content needed from Lou, not drafted by us

The meeting notes ask this page to cover Lou’s heart transplant and his recovery. We have not written it, and we should not. It is his own health and his own story, and a paraphrase of a second hand summary would be both inaccurate and his to object to. The section is built and waiting for his words.

What we need, in whatever form is easiest for him, a voice note is fine: how much he wants said, what he wants it to do for a parent reading it, and whether it belongs on this page at all or sits better in the video above. Some people would rather it were spoken than written.

If published coverage already says it the way he is happy with, the cleanest answer is a short line here and a link out to that piece, which is what the notes suggest with the Fox 5 reference.
